About

I am an artist, experience designer, and educator working across interactive media, live performance, and human-computer interaction research. My practice examines how emotion, attention, and embodied experience are shaped by technological systems, drawing on psychology, feminist theory, and narrative design. I have led IRB-approved interdisciplinary research in HCI and mixed reality, with publications in venues such as the Designing Interactive Systems (DIS) conference and the Journal of Technology in Behavioral Science. This research increasingly informs my work in emotional design for AI systems and HCI.

I am a Visiting Assistant Professor in the School of Information at Pratt Institute and a founding faculty member of the Johnny Carson Center for Emerging Media Arts at the University of Nebraska–Lincoln, where I contributed to curriculum design, program development, and cross-disciplinary collaboration. Across undergraduate, graduate, and professional contexts, I teach research-driven design methods that integrate critical theory, prototyping, and experiential storytelling, with an emphasis on inclusive, discussion-based learning environments that support creative risk-taking and intellectual rigor.

My creative work spans immersive installation, XR, and projection design for theater and live performance, with collaborators and venues including HBO, Meow Wolf, Epic Games, The Public Theater, and La MaMa Experimental Theatre Club. I have also worked as a producer on commercial experiential projects with agencies including Invisible North, Fake Love, The Mill, and Volvox Labs, and have served as a mentor, juror, and panelist for organizations such as SIGGRAPH and the National Endowment for the Arts.

Across these contexts, I approach design and storytelling as cultural and technical practices with implications across social systems, power structures, and ways of knowing. My projects and teaching alike are grounded in the belief that experience design can function as a form of inquiry—one that makes complex social and technological conditions perceptible, negotiable, and open to change.

Bio

Anna Henson is an artist, researcher, and educator whose work sits at the intersection of experience design, emerging technology, and storytelling. She is currently a Visiting Assistant Professor in the School of Information at Pratt Institute, and previously a founding faculty member in Emerging Media Arts at the University of Nebraska. Henson is a creative director, strategist, and producer in interactive and experiential design, and an award-winning projection designer for theater and live performance. Her research explores human experience in digital and mediated contexts through the lenses of behavioral science, design, and narrative. Her peer-reviewed publications appear in Designing Interactive Systems and the Journal of Technology in Behavioral Science. Henson has collaborated with HBO, Technicolor, The Mill, Volvox Labs, Meow Wolf, Epic Games, The Public Theater, and the Carnegie Museum of Art, among others, and has produced brand activations with experiential agencies Invisible North and Fake Love. Her current practice and teaching engage emotional design in the context of AI and HCI, bringing a designer’s concern for agency, legibility, and care to questions about how AI systems model, predict, and respond to human feeling. She is also developing documentary and narrative film projects exploring embodiment, identity, and social change. Her practice is grounded in commitments to community education, queer and women’s storytelling, and independent publishing.
